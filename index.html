About

I am a graduate student at Columbia University in the QMSS program, where I continue developing my interests in economics, quantitative social science, and formal modeling. I graduated from Miami University, where I studied political science, philosophy, and economics and worked as a research assistant. My undergraduate work brought together substantive questions about institutions, behavior, and coordination with increasingly technical interests in econometrics, computational modeling, and mathematical approaches to strategic interaction.

Across my research and independent projects, I am especially interested in the “micro-to-macro” problem: how individual behavioral tendencies, informational frictions, and strategic incentives aggregate into complex outcomes in markets and political systems. That interest has led me toward work in game theory, mean field games, market microstructure, applied econometrics, and related areas of quantitative social science.

More broadly, I enjoy building modular research workflows that translate messy or unstructured information into reproducible analysis. Whether working on archival metadata pipelines, NLP classification, spatial economic analysis, or independent work in mathematical finance and econometrics, I am most motivated by projects that combine substantive curiosity with rigorous empirical or formal tools.

Expectation Regimes in a Krusell–Smith Economy

Research Audit: Sentiment Feedback Asymmetry

Audited and reconstructed an empirical-finance pipeline after uncovering a calendar-availability defect in inherited liquidity measures. Rebuilt daily-to-month liquidity measures, added calendar-integrity tests, manifest-bound outputs, and claim-level provenance controls; the correction overturned the prior headline. Technical reconstruction is complete; provenance and independent replication remain open.

Research-audit / reproducibility case study. Not presented as a causal finance result or distributable replication package. Restricted data are not public.
